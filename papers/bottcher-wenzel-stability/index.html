Research preprint · Matrices and spectral methods

Dimension-independent stability of the real Böttcher–Wenzel inequality

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

The Böttcher–Wenzel inequality bounds the squared Frobenius norm of the commutator of two Frobenius-unit matrices by two. Its real equality cases consist of pairs supported on a common two-dimensional subspace, with traceless, orthogonal restrictions. We give a direct proof that a real pair whose squared commutator norm is \(2-\delta\) lies within \(C\sqrt{\delta}\) of such an equality pair, where \(C\) is independent of the matrix size. The distance uses the product Frobenius norm, neither matrix is required to be symmetric or normal, and no smallness assumption is imposed on the deficit. The exponent \(1/2\) is optimal, already in dimension three. The proof begins with Audenaert’s singular-value refinement, which provides a common subspace of dimension at most eight on which both matrices can be compressed. The retained commutator is orthogonal to the terms linear in the compression errors, so the normalized compressed pair has deficit of order \(\delta\). The remaining fixed-dimensional estimate follows from an explicit second-variation calculation: the deficit grows quadratically in directions transverse to the equality set. Compactness completes this estimate, and the compression argument combines only the constants for dimensions two through eight. The resulting universal constant is existential.
