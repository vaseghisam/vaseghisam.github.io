Certifying conditional dependence among environmental records from CHSH correlations

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

Environmental records can be individually accurate while sharing the same errors. We show how retained coherence can quantify this conditional dependence without reconstructing the joint record distribution. For a balanced two-qubit state supported on \(\operatorname{span}\{\lvert00\rangle,\lvert11\rangle\}\), we derive the sharp compatibility boundary between a retained Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt (CHSH) value, the accuracy of \(R\) designated local binary records, and their conditional total correlation. The full extremum reduces to a convex problem in \(R+1\) Hamming-weight probabilities, and every boundary value has a pure-state quantum realization using only the designated fragments. Its inverse gives the least dependence compatible with Bell data and separate record calibrations. For two records it is explicit; at fixed accuracy, a fixed positive Bell excess requires dependence growing at least linearly with record count. Independent monitoring reaches the zero-dependence boundary in a Lindblad dephasing limit. A shared-memory Lindblad process instead improves record accuracy at constant Bell value, with an exact time after which dependence becomes certifiable. The result quantifies departures from conditional independence in environmental recording, under trusted state-sector and readout assumptions.
