Near-uniform sampling of factored smooth integers in expected polynomial time

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

Given integers 2 ≤ y ≤ X, we consider the generation of an integer chosen nearly uniformly from the y-smooth integers at most X, together with its complete prime factorization. We give an unconditional algorithm using fair random bits whose output law is within total variation 2^(−h) of uniform and whose expected bit complexity is bounded by a fixed polynomial in ⌈log₂ X⌉ + h, uniformly in y. The algorithm requires no prime list, smooth-number counting oracle, saddle-point evaluation or general integer-factorization routine. Its construction combines tilted independent geometric prime exponents with sparse proposals over dyadic integer intervals. An explicitly filtered grid contains a useful tilt without computing it. Uniform smooth-number estimates give polynomial acceptance, and certified finite-precision primitives preserve both the distributional guarantee and the expected work bound. Every returned integer and its displayed prime factorization are exact; the approximation concerns only the sampling distribution. The guarantee concerns expected bit complexity; no practical running-time comparison is asserted.
