Prime extremality and stability for weighted primitive sets

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

A set of integers greater than one is primitive if no member divides another. We prove Kucheriaviy's conjecture that the primes maximize the sum of \(z^{\Omega(n)}/(n(\log n)^z)\) over primitive sets for every \(0<z\leq1\), where \(\Omega\) counts prime factors with multiplicity. The primes are the unique maximizer throughout this interval and remain so through \(z=4/3\). For \(0<z\le1\), the loss in the sum is at least a positive multiple of the weight of the symmetric difference from the primes. The best such multiple is asymptotic to \(\kappa z^2\) as \(z\downarrow0\). We give a formula for \(\kappa\) involving a supremum of convergent prime sums and show that it rounds to \(0.41123\). To obtain \(\kappa\), it suffices to consider replacements that keep the primes below a cutoff and exchange the remaining primes for the semiprimes they generate. A prime can nevertheless be outweighed in its own least-prime-factor class. For infinitely many primes \(p\), the semiprimes with least prime factor \(p\) outweigh \(p\) for every sufficiently small \(z\), depending on \(p\). The proof of the conjecture reweights Alexeev's divisibility flow for the classical Erdős sum.
