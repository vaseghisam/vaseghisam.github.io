Recovery without a declining opponent: a two-state model motivated by the uncanny valley

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

An improvement in overall affinity need not mean that an unsettling response has declined: a positive contribution may instead come to outweigh a growing opponent. This note examines that possibility in a bounded two-state model motivated by the uncanny valley. It distinguishes Mori's appearance continuum from the evolution of a response during a fixed exposure. With separate positive rates for acquisition, loss, opponent recruitment and removal, both states increase from a neutral start. Their ratio is strictly increasing when \(ab\le(a+d)^2\) and has one maximum when \(ab>(a+d)^2\). In the latter regime, a defined interval of observation weights produces an initial rise, a negative excursion and a return above baseline. A worked example and a complete proof describe how this reversal of relative dominance occurs. A comparison with two real linear decay modes establishes a limited response distinction without selecting a unique mechanism. The measurement analysis also separates parameter uniqueness from prediction: exact opponent and mixed-response traces determine the reduced dynamics despite an unresolved scale of the positive component. The state architecture and general mathematical methods have established precedents, which are identified explicitly. The result is a conditional account of what this model permits, with synthetic illustrations and no participant experiments or empirical parameter fits. It does not establish that the latent components describe human familiarity or eeriness.
