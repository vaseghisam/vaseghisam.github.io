Switch distance and the central spectral gap of cubic graphs

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

Guo and Royle classified the cubic graphs whose adjacency spectra avoid the open interval \((-1,1)\). We show that the number of eigenvalues in this interval measures how far a cubic graph is from their class. Let \(G\) be a finite simple cubic graph on \(n\ne6\) vertices, and let \(k\) count its adjacency eigenvalues strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\), with multiplicity. The minimum number of degree-preserving edge switches needed to reach the class on the same vertex set lies between \(k/2\) and \(Ck\), for an absolute constant \(C\). In the bipartite double of the components that need repair, disjoint paths of four-cycles cover all but \(O(k)\) vertices and have \(O(k)\) ends in total, regardless of their lengths. As a consequence, every growing family of cubic graphs with uniformly positive edge expansion has a positive proportion of its eigenvalues strictly inside the interval.
