Removing the logarithmic loss in a lower bound for sum-difference exponents

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

For a finite rational slope family containing \(0\), \(1\) and \(\infty\), the sum-difference exponent measures how much entropy an output projection can have relative to the largest input projection entropy. Tao bounded this exponent from below by \(2-O_k\!\left(\log(2+D)/D\right)\), where \(D\) is the rational complexity of the output relative to the inputs and \(k\) is the number of additional input slopes. For \(k\ge1\), we prove the lower bound \(2-C_k/D\), with an explicit constant depending only on \(k\). The proof retains Tao's evaluated-polynomial construction and adds a covering estimate between two comparable degree-and-coefficient scales. This estimate holds for sets of evaluated values, including when polynomial evaluations coincide. A separate appendix evaluates the corrected folded-entropy functional associated with the three-slope continuous limit. Its infimum is \(\log(2/\sqrt{3})\) for every folding parameter, by the classical sharp entropy Brascamp--Lieb inequality and concentration into a single strip.
