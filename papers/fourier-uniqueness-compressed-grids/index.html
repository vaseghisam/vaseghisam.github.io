Logarithmic regularity and Fourier uniqueness on compressed square-root grids

Sam Vaseghi · Wisehouse Science Communication Lab, Wisehouse AB

Abstract

When do the values of a function and its Fourier transform determine the function? We study this question on the grids \(\Lambda_\beta=\{0\}\cup\{\pm\sqrt n/[\log(e+n)]^\beta:n\ge1\}\). For \(\alpha>1\), let \(\mathcal X_\alpha\) consist of functions whose squared absolute values, and those of their Fourier transforms, are integrable with weight \((1+|x|)[\log(e+|x|)]^\alpha\). If \(\beta>1/2\), simultaneous vanishing on the grid forces zero in every \(\mathcal X_\alpha\). If \(0\le\beta<1/2\), every fixed \(\mathcal X_\alpha\) contains an infinite-dimensional real space of even Fourier-invariant functions with the prescribed zeros. On the critical grid \(\beta=1/2\), uniqueness holds for \(\alpha\ge3/2\), including equality, while the construction of Bondarenko and Seip gives nonuniqueness for \(\alpha>1\) sufficiently close to \(1\). Thus uniqueness on \(\bigcup_{\alpha>1}\mathcal X_\alpha\) holds exactly when \(\beta>1/2\). The proof combines a constrained Neumann spectral estimate with the Kulikov–Nazarov–Sodin localization method, and extends the Bondarenko–Seip kernel estimates to an enlarged tail. The exact critical regularity on \(\beta=1/2\) remains open. The results concern exact infinite sampling; no stability or finite-data reconstruction claim is made.
